# Wireless and remote technology-mod survey: 100 projects (2026-10-03)

## Purpose and method

The user requested broader research before narrowing the remote Federation idea, then explicitly asked to continue
until the catalog reached 100 projects. Three subagents investigated distinct project groups while the parent
checked additional projects and consolidated the evidence. The initial eight projects from the
[first survey](wireless-tech-mod-survey-2026-10-02.md) are retained once each.

Count independent mod projects, not devices, Minecraft versions or loader ports. Addons count as projects but
are labeled as addons; several may belong to one design family. Positive findings and inconclusive/negative
screening are separate. A large project count does not imply the same number of distinct connection designs.
The sample includes major technology mods, transport/storage utilities, smaller specialist mods, historical
implementations and explicitly labeled automation comparisons. It is not a popularity ranking.

Sources are author platform descriptions, official manuals/wikis, and author source repositories. An author
description confirms documented intent, not runtime qualification. Historical mechanics are not generalized to
current releases. Unless stated otherwise, automatic chunk loading, exact operating costs and delivery guarantees
were not established. A platform listing for 1.21.1 does not prove that every mechanism from a different branch is
available in that build. No mod was installed or tested in-game during this survey.

Product proposals remain in [the idea library](../../docs/ideas/remote-federation-connections.md). This is research
evidence, not a decision to copy these mods or implement the candidate features.

## Coverage summary

- **100 independently counted projects:** 90 with evidence of a relevant remote/wireless mechanism, plus ten
  screened projects for which a qualifying mechanism was not established.
- The previous catalog contained 58 projects (49 mechanism references and nine screening records). The final
  expansion adds **42 projects: 41 mechanism references and one screening record**. Entries 01-49 and S01-S09
  retain their original IDs; new entries are 50-90 and S10.
- The 90 mechanism references include direct network links, resource transport/shared storage, local coverage,
  player/remote-computer access, signal communication and explicitly identified delivery/teleportation comparisons.
  They are not 90 interchangeable Federation bridges, nor 90 fully qualified current-version implementations.
- Historical AE2 Wireless Terminal Library and successor Quartz Arsenal are consolidated as lineage references,
  not additional counts. Versions, loaders, and multiple devices within one project are not extra entries.
- Well-known projects anchor the sample. Smaller specialists such as Create: Improve and Wireless Networks are
  explicitly included for distinctive interactions, not described as popularity leaders. Botania is an automation
  comparison with a natural-magic theme.

## Evidence corrections and close-reading notes

- Wireless Utilities is not limited to resource transfer. `TileAENetworkBase.updateConnection()` invokes
  `AEApi.instance().grid().createGridConnection(node, targetNode)` at line 626 of the pinned source. Its
  `getNodeAt()` resolves `BlockPosDimension` through the target world. This establishes actual network topology
  connections and dimension-aware lookup, but does not alone establish every device's cross-dimensional rules.
  The author description separately confirms directional/positional ME and RS machines. The repository owner
  is SirStendec, not the package-name-derived guess Lordmau5.
- A focused recheck of [Wireless Utilities' directional ME implementation](https://github.com/SirStendec/WirelessUtilities/blob/7e2723767829fde7288dc4fa8f52f0795891ec8f/src/main/java/com/lordmau5/wirelessutils/plugins/AppliedEnergistics2/network/tile/TileDirectionalAENetwork.java#L166)
  confirms iteration over a configured volume, compatible-node lookup and automatic connection attempts. This is
  constrained enrollment: powered/active state, node compatibility, color matching, upstream grid acceptance and
  exclusions for certain dense cable parts matter. The directional volume is in the local dimension. The ME base
  also has an optional chunk-loading augment path, not unconditional loading. Do not infer RS dimension support
  from the ME code: the inspected RS positional visitor passes its local world to the target operator.
- ExtendedAE's Hub guide and [implementation](https://github.com/GlodBlock/ExtendedAE/blob/26.1.2-neoforge/src/main/java/com/glodblock/github/extendedae/common/tileentities/TileWirelessHub.java#L39)
  agree on eight ports and connections to hubs or connectors. Setup selects a free port automatically, but endpoints
  are still explicitly paired; this is not coverage discovery. Hub follows the connector's same-dimension, bounded
  reach. Eight ports do not establish eight additional independent 32-channel budgets. This recheck is still of
  `26.1.2-neoforge`, not qualification of a 1.21.1 binary.
- XNet's older author overview still frames wireless as future work, whereas the inspected official 1.16 source
  contains working wireless-router logic. Prefer the versioned source for that claim. Its default per-channel

- Actually Additions injects laser range into its manual; no guessed numeric value is retained. WIP range-modifier

## Design synthesis for Federation (proposals, not decisions)

The expanded sample supports several genuinely different player experiences. Exact Federation topology semantics
must still be agreed independently; none of the examples overrides the existing policy model or the separate
P2P decisions.

| Candidate experience | Strong reference | New question or opportunity |
|---|---|---|
| Explicit remote jump | AE2, Refined Storage, LaserIO | Pair tokens or record an address; show the far endpoint and current availability. This matches the user's first candidate most directly. |
| Hub with individually managed connections | ExtendedAE, Draconic Evolution | One base hub serves several outposts; visible ports/capacity and per-link state make topology tangible. The former directly links ME networks; the latter distributes energy. |
| Membership in a remote group | Integrated Dynamics, AE Additions, Flux Networks | Add another site by joining a group; ownership and join UX matter. Shared energy/storage references do not prescribe Federation resource merging. |
| Local coverage or selected remote targets | Wireless Utilities, Sophisticated Storage | Enroll a local cluster through spatial coverage or target selection. Wireless Utilities provides an actual network-link reference, not just wireless terminal access. |
| Player-built relay mesh | Actually Additions, Botania, local LaserIO | Retain construction and routing across a base/landscape without continuous cable. A remote jump could coexist later, but no combined progression is selected. |
| Infrastructure affected by the world | Immersive Engineering, OpenComputers, XNet | Tower placement, range and upgrades could make connection engineering part of play. Signal/energy limitations in references are not automatically Federation requirements. |
| Selected service/channel publication | XNet, historical Ender IO | A site publishes chosen services rather than assuming complete domain merging. This would require separate product discussion about interaction with Federation policy. |
| Remote endpoint proxy | Entangled | Expose a specific remote endpoint locally instead of connecting entire domains. This is a separate possible feature, not a reinterpretation of the user's cable-like jump idea. |

Useful UX ideas across these candidates:

- Keep pairing/membership, current online state, and resource permissions separately understandable.
- Show destination name, dimension/location, group identity and a concrete failure reason where applicable.
- Provide selection/highlighting tools: LaserIO partner coordinates, Modular Routers target highlighting,
  Wireless Redstone's Sniffer, and Wireless Chargers' coverage visualization address invisible connections.
- Show connection identity in-world through color or item icons, as Ender Storage and Create do.
- Separate reach from availability: cross-dimensional support does not imply force-loading, as LaserIO and
  Modular Routers explicitly demonstrate.
- Create: Improve's graph routing, labels, suggestions and logic operations are an additional UI reference for
  [custom policies](../../docs/ideas/custom-policies.md). This is not evidence that it implements the requested
  bidirectional graph/script conversion or complete script completion/icon support.

The survey expands the idea space; it does not select a device, final name, appearance, recipe, power budget,
range hierarchy, transport algorithm or milestone. In particular, "Federation quantum bridge" remains a temporary
discussion name. Implementation details remain future coding work.

## Additional lessons from reaching 100 projects

These observations supplement the earlier connection models. They are product/research comparisons, not approved
Federation requirements or instructions to choose a transport implementation.

- **Remote identity can remain visible:** CESG exposes Local/Partner views and distinguishes unavailable partners
  from structurally faulty loaded partners. This is useful when considering how independently managed sites appear
  to players. Its filters and extraction locks are its own semantics, not existing Federation policy behavior.
- **Connection ownership need not equal the last placer:** Quick Link documents FTB team and claim ownership;
  AE Wireless Transceiver isolates named channels by teams. Matching a name/color therefore does not necessarily
  grant membership. This supplies concrete UX references for group scope and ownership changes.
- **Endpoints can move:** CC: Wireless Peripheral Sable preserves names and remaps supported moving endpoints;
  Ender Link documents shared storage on contraptions. Stressbound has qualified runtime-anchor support; KineticLink
  still lists full moving-structure integration as planned. Do not combine their claims into universal compatibility.
- **A service request and delivery can be different operations:** Logistics Pipes' remote ordering does not prove
  direct delivery to the player. Create's stock-link signals and Mobile Packages' couriers make that distinction
  visible. This matters when comparing a remote-control service with a transparent Federation link.
- **Persistent connectivity is not the only remote-operation model:** IC2 Classic's historical teleporter batches
  container contents on a pulse; Ender Mail submits an addressed parcel; Digital Items uses unique redeemable item
  IDs communicated through CC:T. These are separate possible transport concepts, not equivalent replacements for
  the user's cable-like jump or an implementation recommendation.
- **Cost can follow usage:** Create: Advanced Logistics consumes pearls for package teleportation; CESG documents
  no transfer-fuel cost while idle. Stressbound reserves capacity per connection and exposes requested versus granted
  capacity. These contrast different operating models without choosing a Federation price or quota system.
- **World conditions and diagnostics can make radio infrastructure legible:** Immersive Intelligence uses bounded
  same-dimension packet relaying with weather-sensitive range; Classic Peripherals models tower height, altitude and
  degraded data near the edge of reach. Signal degradation is a communication-gameplay reference, not a proposal
  to randomly corrupt or lose Federation resources.
- **One interface can serve several contexts:** Link Tablet uses names/icons, group controls, live frequency members,
  handheld screens and mounted displays. These are references for inspecting invisible connections, not a decision
  to add a tablet or wall display to this project.
